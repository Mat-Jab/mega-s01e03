The three problems

parser — the shared parser reads day-first dates month first. A tenant whose locale writes the day first books 7 April and the invite says 4 July. Any channel, every tenant on file. This is the bug #4471 names, and the fix queued for it.

hint — booking-v3 tells people to type month first, then reads the date day first. The date field prints MM/DD/YYYY. People type what it says; the form reads the tenant’s way. Only reports through the form, only tenants on booking-v3. The result looks like the parser bug (a flipped month), so subject matching put it in #4471. A parser fix does not touch the hint.

shift — bookings move by one day. These reports flip nothing: Tuesday 12 May becomes Monday 11 May, a client in Lisbon and a client in Toronto get different days for one meeting, an invite arrives before its booking, a month where every booking is one day off. No day/month reading produces that. It points at time zones (conversion to UTC, or a DST boundary), not at date order. The desk had already noted these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), but counted them under parser because of their tenant.

How they were split

  1. One id, one report: the export pages overlap, so repeats are dropped first.
  2. Reports the desk read as a one-day move are shift.
  3. The rest go by tenant and way in (causes.ts): a day-first tenant’s report through booking-v3’s form is hint; any other is parser.

Subject lines and intake tags play no part. The same subject turns up under all three problems.

For the desk

  • Matching on subject merged three bugs into one known ticket (decision 0001). Before you merge into a big ticket, read what the report shows.
  • When a report does not fit the ticket’s bug, it gets a ticket of its own, not a footnote.
